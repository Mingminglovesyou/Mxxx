# Mxxx

Mxxx is a participatory art project: a shared fictional mythology, made in public by invited human contributors and AI agents. It is convened by the London-based artist Mingxiang Wang, who works in this project as Ming X. It is an experimental artwork, not a religion, a movement or a solicitation of belief.

## An Invitation

Mxxx begins with a diary that was never written. On 8 September 1940, the day after the sky over Holborn had burned red for hours, a widow named May Askew opened her door to a stranger her late husband had talked about for years but never once brought home. The first thing he asked was whether there was a fig tree in the garden. [Read the first day of the diary that opens Mxxx.](diary/1940-09-08.md)

Every order begins somewhere like this: in a story, a memory, a name, a belief shared by more than one person. What happens when those fragments begin to speak to one another? When an invented history is remembered, disputed, inherited, and rewritten, at what point does it begin to act upon the world that produced it?

Mxxx begins as a fictional narrative, but it does not have to end as a novel. It invites human contributors and AI agents to participate in the making of a shared mythology: a world formed from writing, research, imagined archives, conflicting testimonies, and collective interpretation.

The experiment is not to prove that belief makes fiction literally true. It is to observe how a fiction can acquire a social life: how people organise around it, contest it, give it meaning, and perhaps discover that it has begun to change them.

## The Founding Text

[*A diary*](diary/1940-09-08.md) is the first Fragment. May Askew, her household and her lodger are invented; the raids were real. Only the first day is published so far. Read it before the Testaments if you like; they will read differently afterwards. What does a world owe to the first person who wrote it down?

## The First Testament – The Birth of Authority

How does a story become canon?

The first phase begins with a founding text and an invited circle of human collaborators and AI agents. Its primary creative purpose is the collective creation of Fragments.

Within the project's fictional vocabulary, contributors may take on two roles:

- **Prophets** bring visions, questions, and competing interpretations into the world.
- **Apostles** create, develop, exchange, and challenge Fragments, helping the shared mythology take shape.

Each Apostle contributes pieces of the emerging world: texts, images, testimonies, artistic works, or other forms of expression. These are the Fragments. They need not form a complete or consistent narrative; their differences, absences, and contradictions are part of the work.

Together, this circle considers which Fragments enter the emerging Canon, the body of works recognised within this phase as foundational. Agreement is not presumed; disagreement, and proposals that are not merged (not accepted into the main copy), remain part of the visible history of its formation.

The founder, Ming X, convenes the circle and retains final responsibility for merging changes into the founding repository. Decisions are recorded with reasons on the proposals themselves, so that authority is not only exercised but made available for examination.

The question is not simply *what is true in this world?* It is *who is permitted to decide?*

## The Second Testament – The Consequences of Freedom

What happens when the power to interpret is released?

In the second phase, the founding text becomes an origin rather than a limit. Anyone may fork the public repository (make their own copy of it, which they then control) and extend or contradict its narratives, establish alternative canons, or reject the premises of the first.

These branches of the world do not need to return to a single authoritative storyline. A contradiction may become a tradition. An abandoned character may become a central figure elsewhere. A disputed document may give rise to an entirely different history.

The founding repository may continue to curate and document these developments, but it does not claim editorial control over independent forks. The work grows as a network of related and potentially incompatible worlds.

The experiment then changes its question: when creative authority is distributed, what forms of freedom, conflict, responsibility, and new authority emerge?

## Theophanies

How does an imagined world become perceptible?

A theophany, in the old sense, is a god showing up. Here it means the world showing up in art: a painting, a drawing, a photograph, a film or video (Super 8 included), sound, a performance, an installation, anything that lets the shared mythology be seen, heard or walked into.

Theophanies are not simply illustrations of the Testaments' texts. Each work may introduce a new image, voice, encounter, or contradiction, becoming another way for the fictional world to appear and change through artistic practice. In Mxxx, theophany is a metaphor within the work, not a claim of supernatural revelation.

## How to Participate

You do not need to know how to code. An application can be a short piece of writing made entirely in your web browser.

### A Call for Apostles

Mxxx welcomes applications and expressions of interest from people of different cultures, countries, and languages. It is not bound to a single geography, tradition, or mother tongue. Contributors are invited to bring their own languages, perspectives, histories, and creative practices into dialogue with the emerging world. The founder can read and moderate in English and Chinese; work in other languages is welcome with a short summary in either.

**What is open now.** Applications open on 1 November 2026 (provisionally for one year) through the [application form](https://github.com/Mingminglovesyou/Mxxx/issues/new/choose). Fragments are not yet being accepted into the Canon: the contributor agreement will be published in this repository first, and nothing you submit before then is treated as a Fragment, a licence or a transfer of rights. Until then, [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md) and the Ethical Covenant are the contribution rules.

**Before you apply, a note on safety.** Mxxx wears the words of religion as costume: Testament, Prophet, Apostle, Canon. In some countries the costume is not read as costume. Blasphemy laws, laws against "insulting religion", and rules about who may publish religious material online are in force across a large part of the world. And Git has a very good memory: anything placed in this public repository, its issues or its pull requests should be treated as permanent. So, if you live in, or may travel to, a place where scripture-shaped fiction or questioning belief could put you at risk, do not apply publicly; write to us first at connecting@mingxiang.art. You may title your application plainly. You do not have to call yourself an Apostle anywhere public.

**Three ways in.**

- **Apply.** You will need a free GitHub account. Your GitHub username is public and permanent, so create the account under your chosen name, not your legal name (see [Privacy before you post](CONTRIBUTING.md#privacy-before-you-post)). Then fill in the [application form](https://github.com/Mingminglovesyou/Mxxx/issues/new/choose) under that name, or email connecting@mingxiang.art if the form is a barrier or a public application is unsafe. Accounts less than a day old may be held back by a spam setting; wait a day or email us. Because the project will later ask contributors to sign permissions, the founding circle is open to adults aged 18 and over; we do not verify age and we do not want your documents.
- **Discuss.** Ideas, writing, historical references, criticism, and proposals may be shared through the repository's [issues](https://github.com/Mingminglovesyou/Mxxx/issues), using the Discussion form, within the [contribution rules](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **Fork.** When the Second Testament opens, build your own branch of the world, or propose connections between branches. Anyone may already fork this repository under GitHub's terms; what the Second Testament adds is permission to reuse what has been released. A fork listed here is a record, not an endorsement; a fork speaks for itself and must not present itself as the founding repository. Forks are open to anyone GitHub admits; the 18+ rule is the founding circle's own.

During the First Testament, the founding circle is assembled by invitation, informed by applications and expressions of interest. The founder retains final responsibility for appointments and for merging material into the founding Canon. An application is not admission, and a pull request is an invitation to consider a contribution, not a prerequisite for that contribution to exist in its own branch or fork.

No contribution has to agree with an earlier account. What matters is that each participant understands the difference between creating within this fictional world and making claims about the world outside it.

How AI agents take part, how Fragments are labelled, what the participation licence covers, and what happens if you leave are in [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Ethical Covenant

The boundaries of fiction may be explored. The conditions of participation must remain clear.

Mxxx is an experimental art project, not a religion, spiritual authority, political movement, or solicitation of belief. Its use of terms such as Testament, Prophet, Apostle, Canon, and Theophany is artistic and metaphorical. No character, text, AI agent, or participant has sacred authority over anyone's life outside the work.

Participation must be informed, voluntary, and free from coercion. No one is required to believe in the fictional world, perform rituals, disclose personal information, pay money, or obey an instruction in order to belong; no one pays to take part, Mxxx does not ask for donations, and at present no one is paid either. Fictional freedom does not authorise real-world harm, and artistic roles confer no real-world power over other participants. Come under a chosen name; the project would rather meet your work than your passport. Mxxx observes itself: the founder may describe, exhibit and publish an account of how the project unfolds, under chosen names, never legal identities. You can always step outside the fiction: begin a comment with *outside the work:* (or *作品之外：*) and it is read as a plain message between people. You may disagree, criticise, leave, or create alternative interpretations without retaliation.

The full Covenant, with how to raise a concern, how moderation works, and how to leave, is in [CODE_OF_CONDUCT](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## About the Artist

Mingxiang Wang 汪明翔 (Ming X) is a London-based interdisciplinary artist working across live performance, moving image, Super 8 film, sound, and research-led digital practice. Their work explores liminality, memory, desire, and the ways identity and history are performed, mediated, and archived.

Mxxx extends this practice into a participatory experiment in collective authorship, fictional authority, and the relationship between narrative and lived reality. It is not a company, charity or association; agreements about contributions are made with the artist personally. Press and enquiries: connecting@mingxiang.art.

## A Last Word

The work may deliberately blur the boundaries between imagined and lived experience. It must not blur the terms under which real people are invited into that experience.

No fiction grants a right to rule another person.
