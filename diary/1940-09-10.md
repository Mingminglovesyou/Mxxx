**Tuesday 10 September 1940**

It was the square.

Dreadful. They’ve put a rope across the top of Doughty Street and won’t let anyone through. M came back from the mews early, dust all over his coat, and asked how I was as politely as ever.

Tired. Nearly said a good deal more. I was glad to see him come back through the door. Everyone else seemed to be going out to look.

It’s eleven, they were still carrying people out. Seventeen taken to the Royal Free, according to Pike, and some of the nurses dead. I don’t know where he gets his figures. Ambulances, firemen, men from the gas and electric, even the animal people. Tull thought they should have stayed away till the people were out. I don’t see what difference their being there made, after all, we are all heading to the same end.

I heard there’s another bomb hasn’t gone off, in the garden of Mecklenburgh Square. Shelter there emptied.

Fenn says it will go off in its own time. Well, obviously, but I’d rather he hadn’t put it like that. I keep thinking I can hear something. Can’t possibly from here.

~~it will go off in its own time~~

Went up to the rope for a little. People saying there were still people underneath. Somebody kept saying they’d gone down because that was what they were told to do. I couldn’t bear listening to him.

*A woman and a man* arrived in a car in the afternoon and couldn’t get through either. She called up to a window. I don’t know who they were. There was talk of getting through by the mews if you knew the way, though somebody else said a man had been put there too.

Fenn and Pike in my hall this evening, reading from Fenn’s book. Every little thing entered with the time. One entry about a woman’s body being taken out. Pike wanted to know which woman. Fenn wouldn’t say. I went into the kitchen. Had heard enough.

Must get the kitchen clock going properly. Started it but it’s losing. Have washed the cups. Saucepan can soak till tomorrow. Can’t face it tonight.

It will go off in its own time.
