**Wednesday 11 September 1940**

Much better for a little sleep this afternoon; maybe the next step is to adopt a cat to nap with me because I still feel cross if any human being starts talking at me. Tull here twice, though I told her I wanted to lie down. Apparently that meant come back later. Have people not read those portraits of a widow’s life? They are partly true!

M asked whether they’d got all the people out of the three houses. I said I thought so. ‘The ones who went down?’ Yes, those. I don’t know what else I could have said. Nobody tells us anything certain.

M and I had tea together in the garden today, we talked about the war in China and our lives. The sun was out and it felt as though nothing had ever happened.

I don’t know why I was thinking of Mother today and those old Foundling buttons she used to tell us about. Left with babies, so their mothers could claim them again. Long before her time there. I used to worry how anybody would know which baby went with which button, once they’d changed all the names and given them numbers. Edie says she doesn’t remember being told any of this.

Funny what she remembers and what I do. After Mother died everybody kept telling me to look after Edie. Father gone too, and nobody seemed to think I might want looking after. For heaven’s sake, I was a child myself. Not Edie’s fault, of course. (Must write to her, keep putting it off.)

I was not going to cry until I saw his eyes. God, May Askew, seal those tears in a bottle and pull yourself together, please. At a time like this!

We talked about Arthur, like always; the ‘stranger’ I married, the phantom who’s never home. His stubbornness, childishness, and how he could never get to sleep with cold feet. I’d wondered why his feet were burnt when he came back from one of his business trips. M said they hadn’t sealed the hot-water bottle properly. I knew it was not the fight with pirate story acclaimed by Arthur!! Like one of the many stories he brought back with the trip in far east! I was being too kind to bring breakfast to bed for weeks, every single morning, because of him whined about the burn. M apologised. I don’t understand why they needed to.

We missed him after all. And somehow, M and A feel strangely similar sometimes. I couldn’t tell why.
