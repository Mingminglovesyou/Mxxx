# A diary

*Extract from the diary of May Askew, aged 51, widow and landlady, Holborn, London, 8 September 1940.*

**Sunday 8 September 1940**

At last we met. Not that I'd been waiting since noon yesterday.

Arthur was always talking about this business partner of his. Right up until he died, and somehow I never met this person once. I had quite expected somebody from Sussex. Chinese, I thought when I opened the door, though I wouldn't really know. Nobody had thought to mention that.

Somehow I had already had a feeling this Lucas would be different. With all Arthur's bowls and prints about the place, I might have been abroad myself. Mrs Butler used to say our rooms looked 'inspired by the Royal Pavilion'. I wish I'd told her everyone in the square knows that gentleman of hers already has a wife.

Quite surprised how easily he lifted the broken Chinese screen, tall and slim as he is. Tull and the woman upstairs had been at me to put it away, though neither offered to help. He carried it into the cupboard. All my polishing wasted. Arthur was told it was very old when he bought it. I expect he paid too much.

Anyhow, what a strange person Mr Lucas is. The first thing he asked was whether there was a fig tree in the garden. Do I look like someone who's good at trimming bushes? Told him no, there wasn't. I made a ridiculous fuss getting the room ready and not a word about that, or the rent, or the room! Even brought the good cups out, till Tull asked if I meant to do that for every prospective lodger. Put them away again. I wish she'd either help or leave me to it.

He took the back room. I was glad, with the front pane still cracked. One case, brown, and a coat over his arm. Paid a month in notes, up to 8 October. Must finish the entry in the book tomorrow.

Asked what I liked to be called. Said Mrs Askew, and got 'I hope I find you well, Mrs Askew.' Such a solemn and foreign way of saying how do you do. I said quite well, thank you, automatically.

Felt dreadful after last night. Hardly any sleep. The sky was red for hours.

He asked the way to the square, also what used to be there. Another strange topic. Told him about the Foundling land, up at the top of the street and beyond. Perhaps interested in old places. Arthur would have kept him talking for hours.

Under the stairs again tonight. Tull asked where he had come from and got 'From the station.' I was glad I wasn't the one who asked. The two from the top have each left boots outside their room again. Must have a word tomorrow. Somebody will go over them in the dark.
