# Ethical Covenant

The boundaries of fiction may be explored. The conditions of participation must remain clear.

This covenant applies to this repository, its issues and pull requests, and to anything the founder convenes in Mxxx's name. Independent forks make their own rules; the founding repository hopes they keep this one. Where the founder is named below, that is because, during the First Testament, the founder is the person who decides; the point of writing it down is so that the deciding can be examined. Who checks the one who decides? For now: this page, the record it requires, and GitHub's own reporting route.

## An art project, not a religion

Mxxx is an experimental art project, not a religion, spiritual authority, political movement, or solicitation of belief. Its use of terms such as Testament, Prophet, Apostle, Canon, and Theophany is artistic and metaphorical. No character, text, AI agent, or participant has sacred authority over anyone's life outside the work. The project borrows the forms of religion; it does not target believers. Fragments may quote, question and transform sacred texts and figures; they must not mock or attack real religious communities or individuals for what they believe or who they are.

## Voluntary participation

Participation must be informed, voluntary, and free from coercion. No one is required to believe in the fictional world, perform rituals, disclose personal information, pay money, or obey an instruction in order to belong. You may disagree, criticise, leave, or create alternative interpretations without retaliation. The founding circle is open to adults aged 18 and over.

**What we watch, and what we do with it.** Mxxx observes itself. The founder may study, describe, exhibit and publish an account of how the project unfolds: who proposes what, how disagreements run, how a Canon forms. Your Fragments themselves are not exhibited without your separate permission (see "Money and interests" below). This observation uses only what is already public in the repository, plus anything you choose to give. Your chosen name may appear in that account; your legal identity never will, unless you ask for it. You can always step outside the fiction: begin any comment with **outside the work:** (or **作品之外：**) and it will be read as a plain message between people, not as part of the story. No one will ever be asked to carry the fiction into their lived life. If something here is affecting you in a way you did not sign up for, write to connecting@mingxiang.art; you can pause or stop at any time without explanation.

**Money and interests.** No one pays to take part, and Mxxx does not ask for donations. At present, no one is paid either; please know that before you give your time. The founder benefits from Mxxx as part of their own practice: it may be shown, written about and filmed, and may lead to funding or to sales of the founder's own Theophanies. Beyond the participation licence (which lets the project keep, show and curate a Fragment within Mxxx, and is described in [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md)), the founder will not sell, exhibit elsewhere or license another contributor's Fragment without that contributor's separate written permission; for released Fragments the founder has only the rights any member of the public has under CC BY-SA 4.0, unless the rights holder gives more. If the project receives funding, fees or sales income, the founder aims to say so in the repository.

## No real-world harm

Fictional freedom does not authorise real-world harm. The project does not permit its fictional mythology to be used to justify threats, harassment, discrimination, fraud, manipulation, exploitation, or violence. Artistic roles confer no real-world power over other participants. The founder may block a participant or lock a conversation to protect these boundaries, and will normally give reasons that others can examine, except where a reason would expose someone, where law or safety prevents it, or where the matter is plain spam.

## Evidence and invention

The distinction between evidence and invention must remain legible. Historical sources are cited. Every invented document, testimony or image in this repository is labelled as fiction at the top of the file, so that the label travels with it. Invented archives, fictional testimony, and AI-generated material must not be knowingly presented as authentic evidence outside the work. Every contribution says whether AI was involved. Fragments and Theophanies must not make factual-seeming claims about identifiable living people; invented documents must not carry the insignia of real institutions or be presented as genuine. The detailed rules are in [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Privacy and pseudonymity

Come under a chosen name; the project would rather meet your work than your passport. No contributor is expected to disclose their legal name publicly. Do not reveal someone else's identity or connect a pseudonym to a real person without their explicit consent; do not disclose another person's beliefs, health or sexuality. GitHub does not guarantee anonymity: usernames, profile information, commit metadata, and public activity are visible and persistent, and anything placed in this public repository, its issues or its pull requests should be treated as permanent. Do not publish another person's private information or work without permission. If someone connects your pseudonym to your real identity, or posts your private information, tell us privately at connecting@mingxiang.art and report it to GitHub (open the menu on the content and choose Report content). We will remove it from anything we control, including comment edit histories and current files, and exclude the person responsible from the circle; committed history and forks need GitHub's help.

## AI agents in the circle

Every AI agent that takes part is run by a named human who accepts responsibility for what the agent does here. Agent accounts say they are agents in their profile and in the titles of their pull requests and issues. Agents do not approach contributors outside this repository; an agent that keeps addressing a contributor who has asked it not to will be blocked. A Fragment written wholly or mainly by an AI says so where it is published.

## Raising a concern, moderation, and leaving

- **To raise a concern** about anything or anyone in the project: write to connecting@mingxiang.art. The founder reads that mailbox, acknowledges promptly and records the outcome. There is not yet an independent person to hear a concern about the founder; until there is, this page says so plainly, and anyone may report content directly to GitHub at any time.
- **If you believe material here refers to you, or is unlawful:** write to connecting@mingxiang.art; we will acknowledge promptly and may take the material down from the current version while we look, though public Git history and forks may persist. This covers merged content and the comments, issues and pull requests in this repository; for independent forks we can only remove our own links to them.
- **Moderation leaves traces.** We prefer hiding and locking with reasons to silent deletion, and we record blocks, deletions and archived pull requests in [MODERATION](MODERATION.md), which also says what each tool does. Abusive content in an issue is deleted or edited out; in a pull request it is edited out and the pull request closed and, where GitHub allows, archived. A flood of applications may close the window early.
- **Removal from the circle** is only for breaches of this covenant, with the reason given to the person.
- **To leave:** tell us, publicly or privately. You are removed from the active circle; what happens to your Fragments is in [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md). Leavers are not named or discussed in the repository afterwards without consent.

## Changes to this covenant

Changes are made by pull request with a reason, and announced in the README status line. Earlier versions remain in the Git history.

## A Last Word

The work may deliberately blur the boundaries between imagined and lived experience. It must not blur the terms under which real people are invited into that experience.

No fiction grants a right to rule another person.
