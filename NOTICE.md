## Copyright and the Freedom to Create

### Individual copyright, collective Canon

Each human contributor keeps the copyright in their original work. In the UK, and in many countries, copyright can only be transferred by a signed written agreement, and nothing in this project transfers it. Appointment as an Apostle or Prophet, inclusion in the Canon, and editorial collaboration do not transfer copyright to Mingxiang Wang, Mxxx, or other contributors. Permissions (licences) are different: some arise simply by posting on GitHub, under GitHub's Terms of Service, which let others view and fork public repositories and let GitHub use posted content to train AI models (Section D.4, as at April 2026). Beyond those, no licence is granted to anything in this repository today. Mxxx does not presume that an AI agent holds or can grant rights; the person or organisation operating an AI agent is the contributor for all purposes. The project intends that any compilation or database right in the selection and arrangement of the Canon is released under the same CC BY-SA 4.0 terms as the Fragments, so that whole canons can be forked. Because the circle takes part in selecting the Canon, who holds that right, and how it is licensed, will be settled in the contributor agreement.

### The First Testament

Fragments are developed within the founding circle. Because this repository is public, anything merged into it is visible to everyone and may be viewed and forked on GitHub under GitHub's Terms of Service; please treat a pull request as publication. Before any Fragment is merged, each contributor accepts a short participation licence (described in [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md)) that lets the project keep, show and curate the Fragment here and lets other participants build on it within the project. "Show" means display in this repository and the project's own online copies, not exhibition elsewhere. That licence is not a transfer of copyright and is not a Creative Commons release. A submission or pull request is not, by itself, consent to release original artistic work under CC BY-SA 4.0. The contributor agreement will be published before any licence notice is added to this repository, so that GitHub's default rule for contributions to a licensed repository (Terms of Service, Section D.6) is intended never to apply in its place.

### Informed agreement before release

Before any contribution enters the Second Testament's openly licensed collection, the project must obtain and record the necessary permissions from its rights holders. Contributors must understand the permitted uses, including commercial adaptations and the possibility of uses they dislike: this is the moral right of integrity, which a releasing contributor agrees not to enforce against licensed uses. They must also understand that CC BY-SA 4.0 is irrevocable as long as its terms are followed: stopping future publication does not undo rights already granted to the public. Material without adequate permission will not be included in the openly licensed collection. When two or more people make a Fragment whose parts cannot be separated, every joint author must agree before it is released. Every released file will be marked with its creator, year and licence, and the licence will state exactly which files it covers, so that unreleased material is never mistaken for licensed material.

### Credit without forced disclosure

Attribution may use a contributor's chosen pseudonym, in accordance with the contributor's wishes and the licence. The licence does not require a public legal name. Private rights verification may sometimes be necessary, but such information must not be placed in public submissions and is handled separately and confidentially in the project's rights record.

### Rights, sources, and endorsements remain distinct

A contributor may authorise only rights they actually control. Images, film, music, text, quotations, performances, and the faces, voices or private moments of identifiable people may need separate permission from the people concerned: the copyright owner, the performer, or the person shown. Theophanies that show a recognisable person, or that record someone else's performance, need that person's written consent before they enter the Canon; the consent covers any later Creative Commons release, which is permanent and allows commercial reuse. Children appear only with a parent's or guardian's consent. Consents are kept in the private rights record, not in this repository. The CC licence does not grant trade-mark, privacy, data-protection or personality rights (sometimes called publicity rights), and a contributor can only waive their own, to the limited extent the licence needs. ### AI-assisted and AI-generated material

The law differs by country and is changing. In the UK, where a computer-generated work has no human author, the person who made the arrangements for its creation is currently deemed its author (a rule the UK government proposed removing in March 2026, not yet enacted); where a human made the creative choices, ordinary authorship rules apply. US law protects only human authorship, so wholly AI-generated material may have no copyright there and a Creative Commons licence would add nothing to it. The person operating an AI agent signs any agreement, must say how far AI was involved, and must confirm that the AI tool's own terms allow the output to be published here and, if released, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.

This is the proposed licensing framework, not an automatic licence covering anything currently in the repository. The applicable licence and the scope of each release will be clearly identified when approved material is published. The project will adopt a contributor agreement and a rights record before any Fragment is merged, and will not add a licence file to this repository before then.
